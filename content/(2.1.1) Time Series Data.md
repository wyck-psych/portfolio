**An Example of Why Time Series Data Require Special Treatment**
- Let's say that I have a coin that can either land heads or tails. I want to know if the coin is fair (P(heads) = 0.5). After tossing the coin 1000 times, I find that the coin has landed heads exactly 500 times and tails 500 times. On first glance, the coin appears completely fair; the observed proportion of heads is perfectly consistent with a fair coin. Thankfully, I stored the results of my 1000 flips as an ordered sequence of heads and tails. Looking at the data, I see many long strings of heads or tails in a row, which is unusual for a fair coin. After analyzing the data, I find that after each heads, the probability of getting heads again is actually 0.8, and similarly, after getting each tails, the probability of getting tails again is actually 0.8. If the coin was fair, these probabilities should be 0.5. Thus, the coin does not appear to be fair, even though its long run proportion of heads is 0.5! The coin appears to have some magical disdain for switching sides. 


**Anomaly Detection in Time Series Data**
- Anomaly detection problems in time series data may involve one or more sequences. When a single sequence is involved, the problem is sometimes called *change detection* and involves identifying real changes when the variable of interest is noisy. For instance, we might analyze a company's hourly stock price to identify large shifts in value beyond what is expected from noise. 
- When multiple sequences are involved, sequences may be anomalous relative to other sequences in the dataset. For instance, we might analyze the distribution of traffic on a university server throughout the day. Past data indicates a pattern with most of the traffic occurring between 9AM-6PM. If today's sequence shows a large amount of activity at, say, 2AM, then this is anomalous compared to the sequences observed in the past and may warrant investigation. 


**Key Considerations for Analyzing Time Series Data**
- *Sequences may be of differing length*. The method must be able to accept inputs of differing length and output values which can be compared across inputs of differing length.
